## Prologue

To frame a new mathematical construct, a good way is to first list the properties you want the construct to have. Let's do this for numbers. The following properties, according to [1] are the most basic properties of numbers.

### Properties of numbers

1. (Associative law for addition)

    $$
    a + (b + c) = (a + b) + c
    $$  

2. (Existence of an additive identity)

    $$
    a + 0 = 0 + a = a
    $$  

3. (Existence of additive inverses)

    $$
    a + (-a) = (-a) + a = 0
    $$

4. (Commutative law for addition)

    $$
    a + b = b + a
    $$

5. (Associative law for multiplication)
    $$
    a \cdot (b \cdot c) = (a \cdot b) \cdot c
    $$  

6. (Existence of a multiplicative identity)
    $$
    a \cdot 1 = 1 \cdot a = a; \quad 1 \ne 0
    $$  

7. (Existence of multiplicative inverses)

    $$
    a \cdot a^{-1} = a^{-1} \cdot a = 1;\quad  a \ne 0

    $$  

8. (Commutative law for multiplication)

    $$
    a \cdot b = b \cdot a
    $$  

9. (Distributive law)

    $$
    a \cdot (b + c) = a \cdot b + a \cdot c
    $$  

10. (Trichotomy law) For every number \(a\). If $P$ is the collection of positive numbers, one and only one of the following holds:

      $$
    \begin{aligned}
    \text{(i)}\quad & a = 0,\\
    \text{(ii)}\quad & a \text{ is in the collection } P,\\
    \text{(iii)}\quad & -a \text{ is in the collection } P.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

11. (Closure under addition)

    $$
    \text{If } a \text{ and } b \text{ are in } P,\ \text{then } a + b \text{ is in } P.
    $$

12. (Closure under multiplication)

    $$
    \text{If } a \text{ and } b \text{ are in } P,\ \text{then } a \cdot b \text{ is in } P.
    $$
